Drag the garnish on the pan, or use the four sliders. The three offsets are the compositor's own PositionOffset, added to that seat in the frame's own units; size multiplies the seat's own scale and starts at the engine's 1.00x. Dragging is the fast path and the sliders are the one a keyboard can reach. The pan is a crop of the compositor's frame, so a garnish tall enough to overhang it is cut at the pan's edge.

These hide a layer so you can look at what is under it. Preview state only — they change nothing about the drink, and the recipe still owns all four layers, ice included. Ice has no toggle because the layer is parked: it is not loaded or drawn at all for now, so there is nothing here it could be taken out of. It is also why the count in this section's head can never reach four. WHERE THE GARNISH RIDES IS NOT A PREVIEW, and the two halves of this panel are not the same kind of control — item 18. Hiding is you looking under a layer; the order is part of the drink. Behind lets the glass's own edge draw over the garnish, so a slice on the rim reads as tucked behind the glass; in front puts the garnish over that edge, so it reads as sitting on the outside of it. Away from where the two cross they are the same picture.

The glass, the ice and the garnish are set in the recipe editor, not here — this render only reads them. What this page decides is the liquid's colour, where the garnish sits, how big it is, and whether it rides over the drink or under it.
